An iPad booth with a DSLR

An iPad can't talk to a DSLR over USB by itself (Safari and Chrome on iPad have no USB access). There are two ways around that. Pick one.

Option A

iPad + the laptop's camera

The camera plugs into the laptop. The iPad is the guest screen and opens the booth from the laptop over the Wi-Fi, so it never needs USB itself.

  1. 1

    Set up the laptop first

    Install the photobooth4u app on the Windows laptop (Downloads page), sign in with your code and check the camera works in the laptop's own window: Nikon, Canon EOS or Sony Alpha over USB. Sony: set USB Connection to PC Remote. The laptop camera guide covers each brand.

  2. 2

    Put both on the same Wi-Fi

    Use one router at the booth, or turn on the laptop's mobile hotspot and join the iPad to it. No internet is needed.

  3. 3

    Turn on iPad sharing on the laptop

    In the laptop booth open Setup → Share & start and tick Let an iPad use this laptop's camera. The laptop shows an address and a 6-digit PIN, like http://192.168.1.20:47777/ipad.

  4. 4

    Open it on the iPad

    In Safari go to that address and type the PIN. The booth opens. In Setup choose the camera named after your DSLR (it says USB). Tap Share → Add to Home Screen to keep it, and turn on Guided Access (Settings → Accessibility) so guests can't leave the booth.

  5. 5

    Run a test session

    Take a full session. The iPad shows the live picture and countdown, the DSLR fires with its flash, and the photo saves on the laptop's Pictures/photobooth4u folder. Guests' QR codes open the photo from the laptop over the same Wi-Fi.

Good to know

Keep the laptop plugged in with its sleep turned off, and leave its booth window open. The PIN stays the same until you tap Make a new PIN. Printing from the iPad uses AirPrint; to print on the laptop's printer instead, use the laptop's own booth window. Mac laptops work for the iPad screen, but the USB camera control is Windows only for now; on a Mac use a webcam or HDMI capture camera.

Option B

iPad + HDMI capture card

No laptop. The camera's HDMI output goes through a capture card, and the iPad sees it as a webcam. The booth gets live video only, so the DSLR's shutter and flash are not used.

  1. 1

    Get the right gear

    A USB-C iPad with iPadOS 17 or later, an HDMI-to-USB capture card (a "UVC" card, no driver needed), an HDMI cable, and a way to keep the iPad powered (a USB-C hub with power passthrough). A dummy battery keeps the camera on for hours.

  2. 2

    Set the camera

    Switch the camera to video mode with clean HDMI output (no on-screen info), and turn off auto power-off. Use the highest HDMI resolution it offers.

  3. 3

    Plug it in and open the booth

    Camera → HDMI → capture card → iPad. Open photobooth4u.netlify.app in Safari, allow the camera when it asks, then choose the capture card in the Camera menu.

  4. 4

    Light the scene

    Because the flash won't fire, use a ring light or two soft lights in front of the guests. Test the brightness before the event.

Heads up

This relies on iPadOS accepting the capture card as an external camera. It works with many cards, not all. Test with your exact card and iPad before the event.

Problems

If it doesn't work

ProblemTry this
The iPad can't open the addressBoth devices must be on the same Wi-Fi (not guest Wi-Fi with "client isolation"). Check the address on the laptop; it changes if the laptop joins another network. Windows Firewall may ask to allow the app on private networks: tick Allow.
"That PIN isn't right" or "Too many tries"Read the PIN on the laptop again. If the laptop made a new PIN, type the new one. After several wrong tries wait a few minutes.
"iPad sharing is off"Tick Let an iPad use this laptop's camera in Setup → Share & start on the laptop. It stays off until you do, and also stops when the laptop's code is inactive.
No DSLR in the iPad's camera listCheck the camera works in the laptop's own booth window first. Sony: USB Connection must be PC Remote, and close Imaging Edge. Then tap Try Again on the iPad.
The picture freezes or is slowMove the iPad closer to the router, or use the laptop's hotspot. Close other devices that stream video on the same Wi-Fi.
The iPad shows the booth but photos don't saveThe laptop saves them. Keep the laptop's app open and check the folder under Setup → Share & start on the laptop.
Capture card not found (Option B)Try another cable or card. Some cards need the iPad powered separately. Reload the page after plugging in.
